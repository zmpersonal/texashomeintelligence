## The short answer

**No — they moved in opposite directions.**

August 2026 was the hottest month Austin has had in the record we hold: 755 cooling degree-days, the highest of the last 12 months (NOAA NCEI Global Summary of the Month, as of August 2026). HVAC permits went the other way: 1,037 HVAC permits, down 15% month over month (City of Austin Issued Construction Permits (Socrata), as of August 2026).

## The two series, side by side

The assumption is reasonable — it gets hot, systems fail, people replace them — and it is
checkable, which is the only reason it is worth writing about.

| August 2026 | Reading | Against July 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Cooling demand | 755 cooling degree-days | up 17% month over month |
| HVAC permits | 1,037 HVAC permits | down 15% month over month |

In July 2026, Austin issued 1,226 HVAC permits.

## What we are not going to tell you

We cannot say from these two series why they moved apart. A permit is filed days or weeks after the decision to replace a system, and nothing here measures that lag, installer capacity, or how many units simply kept running.

The honest read is narrower and more useful: **cooling demand is not a live indicator of HVAC work being started.** If you are timing a replacement and assuming the rush follows the thermometer, the filing record does not support that.

## Why it might matter to you

The month everyone expects installers to be busiest is not the month the filings peak. If you are getting quotes, that gap is the part worth knowing — and it is measurable, which is more than can be said for most advice about when to call someone.
