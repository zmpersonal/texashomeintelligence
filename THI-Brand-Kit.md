# Texas Home Intelligence — Brand Kit & Brand System

**Version 1.0 · Definitive edition · Prepared for logo designer, UI/UX designer, copywriter, social designer, and front-end developer.**

This is a single, opinionated brand system — not a mood board of options. Where the project already made a decision (entity name, positioning, navy + warm accent, AHI-as-separate-social-property), this kit builds on it. Where it left an axis open, this kit closes it with one recommendation and the reasoning behind it.

The one idea every choice serves: **Texas Home Intelligence knows what Texas homeowners need to know about their home, right now — and can be trusted to say it plainly.**

---

## 1. Strategic Analysis — What This Actually Is

**What it does.** Texas Home Intelligence (THI) continuously ingests Texas-specific data — weather and storms (NOAA/NWS), building permits, energy load, air quality, drought, property and cost signals — and translates it into plain-language intelligence about a specific home or ZIP: what's happening, what it means, and when to act. It also publishes free homeowner tools (cost calculators, QuoteReady brief, dashboard) and authoritative service knowledge (roofing, HVAC, plumbing, etc.).

**Core value proposition.** *You don't have to assemble the picture yourself.* Weather apps, permit portals, contractor sites, and Google each hold one fragment. THI is the only place that fuses them into a single, current, home-specific read — with the data source and freshness shown, so you can trust it and act.

**Why not the alternatives:**
- **vs. Google / a weather app** — those give raw fragments and generic forecasts. THI gives *interpretation tied to your home*: "hail exposure elevated this week; roofs 15+ yrs should be inspected." Translation, not lookup.
- **vs. a contractor marketplace (Angi / HomeAdvisor / Thumbtack)** — those exist to route you to whoever paid for the lead. THI is an *independent intelligence resource first*; help finding a pro is optional, downstream, and clearly separated from the objective data.
- **vs. a home-maintenance blog** — blogs are static and national. THI is live, local, and sourced.

**Why return weekly / bookmark it.** Conditions change: a storm passes, drought stage shifts, permit costs move, HVAC stress spikes in August. The Home Dashboard and ZIP readings are a recurring "how's my home doing" check — the utility of a dashboard, not the one-and-done of an article.

**Why it's credible.** Every number carries its **source and timestamp**. Estimates are labeled estimates with ranges and confidence, never false precision. Facts are deterministic — no AI-invented numbers. This is the entire moat for the #1 business goal (being cited by AI answer engines): *models cite sources that expose sourced, structured, current facts.*

**Emotional response, in the first 2–3 seconds:** *"This is the serious, trustworthy place. It knows my area. It's current. I'm in control now."* Calm competence — the feeling of a good instrument panel, not an alarm.

**What users must trust THI to do:** be accurate, be current, show its sources, not cry wolf, and not let a paid recommendation masquerade as objective advice.

**How the pieces fit under one brand.** Feeds → Dashboard → Alerts → Local Risk → Tools/Reports → Recommendations are all expressions of one operation: *sense the local conditions, translate them, and tell the homeowner what matters.* The brand system's job is to make that pipeline legible — especially the line between **objective intelligence** and **commercial recommendation.**

**Category placement (see §2):** **Home Intelligence.**

## 2. Brand Position

**Brand Purpose.** So that no Texas homeowner is blindsided by something their home was already telling them.

**Brand Promise.** A clear, current, sourced read on your home and your area — and a straight answer on what to do about it.

**Positioning Statement.**
> For Texas homeowners who want to stay ahead of what their home needs, **Texas Home Intelligence** is the independent home-intelligence platform that turns live local data — weather, risk, cost, and property signals — into plain-language guidance you can trust, because every reading shows where it came from and how current it is.

**Primary Value Proposition.** Everything affecting your Texas home, read for you and kept current — in one place you can trust.

**Brand Differentiator (defensible, hard to copy).** The **fusion + translation + provenance** loop: many live Texas data sources, merged and interpreted at the home/ZIP level, with source and freshness shown on every reading. A blog can't be live; a weather app can't be home-specific and multi-domain; a marketplace can't be independent. THI can be all four, and can prove it.

**Brand Category — the one to own: "Home Intelligence."**
Chosen over the alternatives on purpose:
- *Home risk intelligence / home-risk* — too narrow and too alarmist; risk is one input, not the whole product, and it drifts toward an insurance/fear frame the brand must avoid.
- *Property intelligence* — accurate but reads B2B/investor/appraiser, not homeowner.
- *Local property monitoring* — "monitoring" leans surveillance and implies a device.
- *Homeowner data / Texas homeowner data* — "data" is the raw material, not the promise; homeowners want the *answer*, not a dataset.

**Home Intelligence** is broad enough to hold weather, cost, risk, maintenance, and property under one roof; it's already the name; it's the plainest description of the value ("intelligence about your home"); and it gives THI a category it can define and own as the entity AI systems learn to cite. Everywhere the brand explains itself, the sentence is: *"Home Intelligence means live, local, sourced information about your home and what it needs."*

## 3. Audience

**Primary audience — the Texas homeowner.**
- **Age:** 30–65, weighted 35–58. Established enough to own; engaged enough to maintain.
- **Profile:** Owner-occupants of single-family homes in Austin/San Antonio (then Houston, DFW). Mixed but skewing mid-to-upper home values — enough equity that a roof, foundation, or HVAC decision is a five-figure event worth researching.
- **Tech sophistication:** Comfortable with dashboards, Zillow, banking apps, weather apps. They don't need hand-holding; they resent being talked down to.
- **Attitude to maintenance:** Responsible but time-poor. They want to do the right thing at the right time and *not* overspend or get caught off guard.
- **Attitude to contractors:** Wary. They've been upsold, ghosted, or storm-chased. They crave a neutral read before they call anyone.
- **Attitude to risk:** Pragmatic, not anxious. They want signal, not sirens. Alarmism reads as a scam tell.
- **What makes them skeptical:** unsourced claims, false precision, anything that feels like a lead-gen funnel wearing a data costume.
- **What establishes authority:** visible sources, timestamps, ranges with confidence, "we don't do repairs" honesty, consistent naming, real local specificity ("Austin," "78704," "Hill Country clay soils").
- **What gets attention:** a current, specific, home-relevant fact ("hail probability elevated in your ZIP this week").
- **What makes them return:** the dashboard as a habit; alerts that proved useful once.
- **What makes them share:** a clean, surprising, *local* result they can compare with neighbors — the share-card system (§10).
- **What makes them enter an address/ZIP:** a concrete promise of value, "no account required," and obvious data seriousness that signals their info is respected — trust *before* the field, not after.
- **Valuable vs. gimmicky:** valuable = sourced, specific, actionable, current. Gimmicky = a made-up "score" with no methodology. The methodology link is what converts gimmick → instrument.

**Primary persona — "The Prepared Owner."**
> Mid-40s, owns a 2000s-era home in South Austin. Checks the weather radar before storms, keeps a mental list of "things I should probably deal with," and got burned once by a roofer after a hailstorm. Doesn't want to become a home-maintenance hobbyist — wants a trustworthy read so the *big* stuff (roof, HVAC, foundation, flood) never surprises them, and so that when they do hire someone, they walk in informed. Will bookmark anything that reliably tells them "here's what actually matters this month," and will ignore anything that feels like it's trying to sell them.

**Secondary audiences (never displace the homeowner):**
1. **Search engines & AI answer engines** evaluating THI as a citable authority. They "read" the same trust signals the skeptical homeowner does — exposed sources, structured facts, freshness, consistent entity naming — which is why designing for homeowner trust *is* designing for citation.
2. **Home-service companies** who may eventually receive leads or sponsor. They are served in a clearly walled-off commercial layer (§9, §15) that never contaminates the objective intelligence.

## 4. Brand Personality

Eight attributes, ranked. Each says what it means **visually** and **verbally**.

1. **Authoritative** — the anchor. *Visual:* editorial serif headlines, deep navy, generous structure, sources on everything. *Verbal:* declarative, sourced, no hedging-as-filler.
2. **Trustworthy / Transparent** — authority without transparency is just assertion. *Visual:* visible source + timestamp on every reading; clear Data→Recommendation→Sponsored tiers. *Verbal:* states confidence and limits plainly.
3. **Precise / Analytical** — an instrument, not a pundit. *Visual:* monospaced numerals, tabular alignment, ranges not fake exactness, restrained charts. *Verbal:* specific figures, defined terms, "estimated," "as of."
4. **Calm / Situationally Aware** — signal, never siren. *Visual:* muted status colors used only as small indicators; no flashing, no radar-red interfaces. *Verbal:* measured even when urgent (§13).
5. **Local / Texan** — earned specificity, not costume. *Visual:* warm caliche-amber accent, real Texas neighborhoods and skies, ZIP/coordinate cues. *Verbal:* names places, climates, and local rules.
6. **Modern / Engineered** — current technology, quietly. *Visual:* clean grid, IBM Plex system, precise iconography, subtle motion. *Verbal:* plain modern English, no jargon theater.
7. **Premium / Restrained** — quality signaled by discipline. *Visual:* whitespace, one accent used sparingly, no gradients-as-decoration, no stock-photo gloss. *Verbal:* concise; every word earns its place.
8. **Grounded / Slightly Rugged** — Texas competence, not slick SaaS. *Visual:* mineral tones, sturdy type, a survey-instrument logo language; texture used at a whisper. *Verbal:* practical, direct, unpretentious.

**Brand Personality Spectrum** (▮ marks the target):

```
Authoritative      ▮───────────────  Casual
Polished           ────▮────────────  Rugged   (mostly polished, a grounded edge)
Technical          ──────▮──────────  Approachable (technical made legible)
Premium            ───▮─────────────  Mass-market
Local              ─▮───────────────  National
Neutral / gender-inclusive  ▮──────  Masculine   (grounded, NOT macho)
Serious            ────▮────────────  Playful  (serious with dry warmth)
Institutional      ─────────▮───────  Entrepreneurial (independent, not bureaucratic)
Modern             ──▮──────────────  Traditional (modern, editorial gravitas)
Analytical         ───▮─────────────  Emotional
```

**Explicitly NOT:** cowboy/Western kitsch · contractor-badge · cheap · alarmist · governmental · sterile generic SaaS · generic AI · wellness · over-corporate · weather-app clone.

- **The interface is cool and quiet; warmth is an accent, not a wash.** Navy + mineral neutrals carry ~90% of the surface; amber appears in small, deliberate places.

## 6. Typography

The type system does real strategic work: an **editorial serif** for authority (the thing AI-citation depends on), an **engineered sans** for legible dense UI, and a **monospace** for numbers so data reads like an instrument. All three are open-license and on Google Fonts — fast, licensable, production-safe. None is Inter.

- **Display / Headline — Newsreader** *(serif, Google Fonts).* Journalistic, credible, made for reading. It signals "we publish intelligence," puts THI in the company of the FT/Economist/Bloomberg mental model, and hard-differentiates from all-sans SaaS and Inter-default AI startups. Used for marketing/editorial headlines, page H1–H2, report and share-card headlines.
- **Interface / Body — IBM Plex Sans** *(Google Fonts).* Engineered for technical products, highly legible on dashboards and mobile, distinctive without being loud. Carries all UI, body copy, labels, buttons.
- **Data / Numerals — IBM Plex Mono** *(Google Fonts).* Tabular figures for scores, readings, ranges, timestamps, coordinates, ZIP codes, and source labels. Monospace makes columns align and makes data feel measured. (Plex Sans + Plex Mono share a skeleton, so they pair seamlessly.)

**Why this specifically for THI:** the brand's job is to be *trusted as an authority* (serif earns that), *read as an instrument* (mono earns that), and *legible under density* (Plex Sans earns that) — while looking like nothing else in the category. Color carries the "Texas"; type carries the "intelligence."

**Nuance rule:** Newsreader is for *editorial* headlines (marketing pages, reports, share cards, top-level H1/H2). Inside the dashboard, where a serif can feel too literary, **card titles and in-app headings use IBM Plex Sans SemiBold** — keeps dense UI crisp.

**Weights:** Newsreader 400 / **500** / 600. Plex Sans 400 / **500** / **600** / 700. Plex Mono 400 / 500.

**Treatments:**
- *Headline:* Newsreader 500, tight leading (1.05–1.15), sentence case.
- *Body:* Plex Sans 400, 1.6 leading, max ~68ch measure.
- *Dashboard number:* Plex Mono 500, `font-feature-settings: "tnum" 1;`, leading 1.0.
- *Label / eyebrow:* Plex Sans 600, uppercase, `letter-spacing: 0.08em`, Slate 700.
- *Button:* Plex Sans 600, sentence case.
- *Caption / data source:* Plex Mono 400, Slate 500, e.g. `Source: NOAA · as of 08/24 14:00`.

**Type scale — Desktop → Mobile**
| Token | Face / weight | Desktop px/lh | Mobile px/lh |

| display-xl | Newsreader 500 | 56 / 60 | 40 / 44 |
| h1 | Newsreader 500 | 40 / 44 | 30 / 34 |

## 7. Logo & Symbol Direction

**One recommended territory: "The Meridian Mark" — a survey/benchmark emblem.**

The existing mark (star + sun + window) risks Texas-star kitsch and reads busy at small sizes. The recommended replacement owns a territory none of the competitors can: **place + measurement + monitoring**, drawn from the language of land surveying and geodesy — the most credible visual vocabulary for "we know precisely where your home is and what's happening to it."

**The concept.** A geometric **survey benchmark / registration mark**: a bold ring (or rounded square) with four fine **registration ticks** at the cardinal points (N/E/S/W → situational awareness, monitoring, "all directions covered"). At the center, a minimal **gabled roofline** — two strokes forming a peak, *not* a full house outline — with a single **filled node** at the apex in Caliche Amber (the home / the data point / "you are here"). Monoline, precise, confident.

**What it communicates:** precision, location, coverage, property, and current-awareness — authority you can feel before reading the wordmark. It deliberately avoids every listed cliché (no full house, no roof-check, no shield, no brain, no robot, no circuit nodes, no tourist star).

**System behaviors:**
- **Favicon / 16–32px:** drop the roofline detail; keep the ring + four ticks + amber center node. Reads clean at 16px.
- **App icon:** the full mark centered on a Meridian Navy rounded square, amber node.
- **Monochrome:** entire mark in a single ink; amber node becomes a solid dot.
- **Embroidery / print:** monoline construction with ≥1.5px relative stroke stitches cleanly; provide a 2-color version (navy + amber) and a 1-color.
- **Metro editions:** the *same* mark; the four ticks stay constant, and a metro is indicated by the wordmark ("Austin Home Intelligence") plus, optionally, one tick rendered solid to act as a subtle locator. No per-city redraw.

**Wordmark:** "Texas **Home**Intelligence" set in **IBM Plex Sans** (SemiBold), "Texas Home" in Ink/Navy and "Intelligence" in Caliche Amber — preserving the established color logic, but on a cleaner, more engineered letterform than a decorative script. Mark sits left of the wordmark, capped at 28px in the header (as already wired). Provide horizontal lockup (default), stacked lockup (share cards, app), and mark-only.

**Clear space:** minimum = the height of the mark's center node on all sides. **Minimum sizes:** mark-only 16px; full lockup 120px wide.

## 8. Visual Design Language

The interface should feel like a **well-made instrument panel**: dense but calm, precise but readable, authoritative but not intimidating. Density is handled by *rhythm and alignment*, not by shrinking or crowding.

- **Corner radius — measured, not bubbly.** Cards 8px, buttons/inputs 6px, chips/small 4px, data cells 2px, status pills fully rounded. Philosophy: geometry that feels engineered.
- **Cards.** White surface, 1px `Line` border, 8px radius, `shadow-sm`. Elevation comes from border + hairline, not heavy drop shadows.
- **Borders & dividers.** 1px `Line`; inner rules `Line Soft`. Borders do more work than shadows.
- **Shadows — restrained, two levels.** `sm: 0 1px 2px rgba(14,23,38,.06)`; `md: 0 4px 12px rgba(14,23,38,.08)`. Dark panels use an inner hairline instead of shadow.
- **Spacing.** 4px base; scale 4·8·12·16·20·24·32·40·48·64·80. Consistent 8px vertical rhythm.
- **Density.** Information-dense with air: tight number leading, generous body leading (1.6), clear section spacing. Group related readings into cards; never a wall of undifferentiated stats.
- **Icons.** Phosphor Icons, regular weight, ~1.5px stroke, 20/24px (see §12).
- **Maps.** Custom low-saturation mineral base (pale slate land, muted water, navy/slate labels). The home is an amber pin; data overlays use status colors at low opacity. Never the default Google Maps look (see §17).
- **Charts.** Minimal, no chartjunk: Signal Blue primary series, navy/slate secondary, `Line Soft` gridlines, Plex Mono labels, status colors only for threshold bands. Sparklines everywhere trends matter (see §17).
- **Data-viz treatment.** Ranges shown as ranges; confidence shown explicitly; axes labeled in mono; always an "as of" + source line.
- **Photography.** Documentary Texas realism, graded cool-shadow/warm-highlight (see §11).
- **Illustration.** Sparse, technical: line diagrams, cross-sections, map/topo motifs — never spot-illustration mascots.
- **Texture.** At a whisper only: a faint topographic contour or survey-grid watermark on dark panels/share cards, ≤4% opacity. Never on content surfaces.
- **Gradients.** Avoid decorative gradients. Permitted: a single deep navy → depth-navy vertical gradient on dark hero/share panels only.
- **Motion.** Purposeful and quick (150–250ms, ease-out). Numbers count up on first load; sparklines draw once; status changes cross-fade. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No ambient looping animation.
- **Hover.** Cards lift to `shadow-md` + 2px amber top keyline on actionable cards; links gain amber underline; buttons darken one step.
- **Loading.** Skeleton blocks in `Mist` with a subtle shimmer; a small "Fetching latest readings…" line in mono. Never spinner-only.
- **Status indicators.** Dot + label chips (§18); left-border on alert cards; sparkline color for trends.
- **Alerts.** Inline cards with a status left-border + tint bg (§9). Toasts only for user actions, not risk news.
- **Tooltips.** Dark navy popover, `#E8EDF4` text, 6px radius, one hairline; used for definitions and "what feeds this."
- **Source citations.** Persistent, quiet, always present: mono, Slate 500, `Source: … · as of …`, with a link to methodology.
- **Freshness indicators.** A small pill: green dot + "Updated 2h ago" when fresh; Slate + "Updated 6d ago" when stale; never hide staleness.

**How it stays authoritative without intimidating:** one strong headline per view, one primary action, sourced numbers grouped into calm cards, plain-language interpretation beneath each reading, and color used as *quiet signal*. The homeowner always sees a sentence telling them what the number means and what (if anything) to do.

## 9. Dashboard Brand System

The dashboard is where the brand lives or dies. Its central job is to make the **information ladder** unmistakable:

**Data → Analysis → Estimate → Recommendation → Sponsored/Commercial**

Each rung has a fixed visual signature so users always know which they're reading:

| Rung | Signature | Type | Color |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data** | Raw reading + source tick | Plex Mono value; mono `Source · as of` | Neutral (Ink/Slate) |
| **Analysis** | Plain-language sentence under the number | Plex Sans body | Ink |
| **Estimate** | Value with **"Est."** tag, a **range**, and a **confidence** chip | Plex Mono + Plex Sans tag | Amber "Est." tag, neutral value |
| **Recommendation** | Action card with a verb and a "why" | Plex Sans, card-title 600 | Meridian Navy accent / navy CTA |
| **Sponsored / Commercial** | Boxed in **Sponsor Sand**, hairline frame, "Sponsored" mono label, navy CTA | Plex Sans | Commercial palette ONLY (§5) |

**Overall Home Intelligence Score.** A single 0–100 (or A–F) headline number in `data-xl` Plex Mono, on a dark navy panel, with a one-line plain read ("Good — one item worth planning"), a status color *ring* around the number, a sparkline of its trend, and a "How this is calculated" link. The score never appears without its methodology link — that link is what turns it from gimmick into instrument.

**Per-domain cards** (Weather risk · Flood · Fire · Hail · Heat · Air quality · Allergies · Power outages · Internet outages · HVAC stress · Maintenance · Property alerts · Local costs). Each card is a fixed template:
```
[icon] DOMAIN LABEL                        [status dot + word]
──────────────────────────────────────────
  READING            [Plex Mono, big]      ┌ sparkline ┐
  one-line analysis  [Plex Sans]           └───────────┘
──────────────────────────────────────────
  Source: NOAA · as of 08/24 14:00      [methodology ↗]
```
- **Current conditions** = the big mono reading; **historical trends** = the sparkline + an expandable chart.
- **Recommendations** appear as a distinct action row *below* the divider, never blended into the reading.
- **Local costs** always carry the "Est." tag + range + "how we estimate this."
- **Data freshness** is the freshness pill top-right of each card; stale cards show it in Slate, not hidden.
- **Data sources** are on every card (mono line) with deeper detail behind the methodology link.
- **No-data** state uses the Unknown gray + hatch and says why ("No sensor coverage for this ZIP yet").

**The rule that protects the whole brand:** sponsored/commercial content is *always* in the Sponsor Sand box with its hairline and label, and *never* borrows status colors, the score ring, Signal Blue, or amber. Objective intelligence and commercial recommendation must never share a visual language.

## 10. Shareability & Social Identity

The share system is the growth engine for AHI (the social-first property) and for THI's word-of-mouth. Goal: a THI/AHI data object should be **recognizable before the logo is read** — the way a Spotify Wrapped card is unmistakable without copying its look.

**The signature (what makes it recognizable at a glance):**
1. **Deep navy field** (Depth Navy) with a faint survey-grid/topo watermark.
2. **One dominant number** in Plex Mono — the score or the single headline stat — with a status-colored ring.
3. **Location, large and proud** in Newsreader ("78704 · South Austin").
4. **A single amber accent** — the ring, an underline, or the mark's node.
5. **The Meridian Mark + wordmark** locked bottom-left; **date + source** bottom strip in mono.

**Card variants & layout:**
- **ZIP score card** — big number center, location above, one-line read below, freshness + source strip, URL bottom-right.

- **Location prominence:** second only to the number.
- **Date/freshness:** always present, mono, bottom strip.
- **Source:** always named on the card ("NOAA · NWS").
- **CTA/URL:** `austinhomeintelligence.com` (AHI social cards) or `texashomeintelligence.com/78704` — bottom-right, quiet, mono.

## 11. Photography & Imagery

Imagery must reinforce **local property intelligence**, never generic home-service stock.

**Direction — documentary Texas realism, graded cool-shadow / warm-highlight** (navy in the shadows, caliche warmth in the light), consistent across the brand.

**Use:**
- **Texas homes / neighborhoods:** real Austin/SA/Texas streets and roofs — ranch, mid-century, contemporary; mixed, lived-in, specific. Golden-hour or clean overcast.
- **Aerial / oblique parcel imagery:** rooftops from above, neighborhood grids, parcel patterns — the "we see your property in context" view. This is a signature; use it often.
- **Weather / storms / heat:** big Texas skies, approaching cells, heat shimmer, drought-cracked ground — atmospheric and real, never catastrophic disaster-porn.
- **HVAC / maintenance / home systems:** clean, close, respectful detail shots (a condenser, a roof valley, an attic) — as evidence, not as a repair ad.
- **Maps / data-viz:** the branded map and chart styles count as primary imagery.
- **Homeowners:** used sparingly, candid, at-home, un-posed; the home and its context are the subject more than faces.
- **Contractors:** rare, and only ever shown as *the homeowner's informed choice* — never smiling-handshake stock.

**Avoid:** smiling contractor/handshake stock · staged suburban "perfect family" · cowboy hats, boots, longhorns, Alamo tourism · brochure-glossy interiors · red-radar weather-app screenshots · alarmist disaster imagery · AI-render tells (six-fingered, plastic, uncanny).

**Treatment:** subtle unified grade; occasional data overlay (a status chip or reading) pinned onto a photo to fuse "place + intelligence." Corners match card radius (8px) when framed.

## 12. Iconography

- **Library:** **Phosphor Icons** (open-license). Chosen for its multiple weights, which let status escalate by *weight* (regular = normal, bold/fill = severe) and for a complete set covering weather, home systems, and status. *(Lucide is the acceptable fallback if a lighter dependency is required.)*
- **Stroke weight:** ~1.5px at 24px; regular weight default.
- **Shape:** rounded-geometric — engineered but not cold. Consistent with 6–8px UI radii.
- **Filled vs outline:** outline default; **fill reserved for active/selected and Severe status** (meaningful, not decorative).
- **Complexity:** low. Legible at 16px. No detailed pictograms.
- **Custom icons:** only where the library lacks a home-specific concept (e.g., "roof stress," "HVAC load") — drawn to match Phosphor's grid and stroke so the set stays coherent.
- **Weather / risk icons:** Phosphor weather set, colored by status only (a hail icon is Ink until it's Elevated).
- **Home-system icons:** consistent line style across roofing/HVAC/plumbing/electrical.
- **Status icons:** dot + optional glyph; escalate by weight and color together (never color alone).

## 13. Voice & Tone

**Voice (constant):** the calm, precise, local expert who respects your time and never oversells. Plain modern English. Specific over clever. Sourced over asserted. Confident enough to say "we don't know yet."

**Tone (varies by context) — with examples:**

**Normal dashboard reading**
- ✅ "Air quality is good in 78704 today — AQI 38. No action needed."
- ❌ "Breathe easy, friend! The air is absolutely gorgeous today! 🌤️"

**Good news**
- ✅ "Your home's in good shape this week. One item worth planning — see below."
- ❌ "Everything's PERFECT! Nothing to worry about at all!"

**Moderate risk (Watch)**
- ✅ "Hail is possible in your area this week. If your roof is 15+ years old, it's worth an inspection before the season peaks."
- ❌ "⚠️ HAIL THREAT DETECTED — your roof could be at risk!"

**Severe risk**
- ✅ "Severe storms are expected in 78704 tonight, with damaging hail likely. Secure loose outdoor items and stay in. Source: NWS, as of 3:10 PM."
- ❌ "DANGER! Catastrophic hail incoming — protect your home NOW before it's too late!!!"

**Maintenance recommendation**
- ✅ "Your HVAC has run under heavy load for 12 days straight. A filter check now is a cheap way to avoid a mid-August failure."
- ❌ "Your AC is going to break! Don't wait — act immediately!"

**Cost estimate**
- ✅ "Estimated roof replacement in Austin: **$14,000–$21,000** for asphalt shingle. This is a THI estimate from permit values and current material costs — not a quote."
- ❌ "A new roof costs $17,500." (false precision, no source, no "estimate")

**Data uncertainty**
- ✅ "We don't have flood-sensor coverage for this ZIP yet, so this reading is limited. Here's what we do know."
- ❌ [silently showing a confident number anyway]

**Service recommendation (commercial)**
- ✅ "Want a second opinion from a local pro? We can connect you with roofers in Austin. This is optional — and it's how we keep the data free."
- ❌ "You NEED to call a roofer today! Get quotes now!"

**Alert (notification)**
- ✅ "Heads-up: severe storms in 78704 tonight. Tap for what to do."
- ❌ "🚨🚨 URGENT ALERT 🚨🚨"

**Social content**
- ✅ "South Austin's hail season is ramping up. Here's how 78704 compares to the rest of the metro this week."
- ❌ "You WON'T BELIEVE which Austin ZIP is the riskiest 😱"

**Methodology**
- ✅ "The Home Intelligence Score blends weather risk, property age signals, and local cost pressure. Here's exactly how, and where each input comes from."
- ❌ "Our proprietary AI analyzes thousands of data points!"

**Lead-gen CTA**
- ✅ "Get matched with vetted Austin roofers — free, no obligation."
- ❌ "LIMITED TIME: Roofers standing by! Click now!"

**Rule:** urgency comes from *specificity and sourcing* ("severe storms tonight, per NWS"), never from punctuation, caps, or fear. If it reads like a scam text, it's wrong.

## 15. Trust Architecture

Trust is the product. It is communicated both visually and verbally, and it is *layered*: essentials on the card, depth behind an expander.

**On the card, always visible:**
- **Source** (named: "NOAA," "NWS," "City of Austin permits").
- **Freshness** ("Updated 2h ago" / "as of 08/24 14:00").
- **Estimate labeling** ("Est." tag + range) whenever a value is derived, not measured.
- **Status** (dot + word) and a **one-line plain read**.

**Behind an expander / on `/methodology/`:**
- Full **methodology** (how the score and each index are computed, versioned).
- **Historical data** and how far back it goes.
- **Confidence** detail and what drives it.
- **Limitations** (coverage gaps, sample sizes, known caveats).
- **How recommendations are generated** and the line between analysis and commercial suggestion.
- **Sponsorships & commercial relationships** — disclosed clearly and consistently.

**Visual grammar of trust:**
- The **mono source line** is a persistent, quiet fixture — its constant presence *is* the trust signal.
- **Estimates** always wear the amber "Est." tag + range; measured data never does. The homeowner learns the difference instantly.
- **Commercial** content is quarantined in the Sponsor Sand box (§9) with an explicit "Sponsored" label — it cannot be mistaken for objective intelligence.
- **Freshness** is never hidden; stale is shown in Slate, honestly.

**The objective:** THI reads as an *independent intelligence resource whose recommendations can be trusted* — even though the business monetizes home-service leads — precisely because the objective layer and the commercial layer never share a visual or verbal language, and because sources and estimates are always labeled.

## 16. Brand Architecture

**Recommended model: an endorsed / branded-house hybrid, with Texas Home Intelligence as the master brand and system-of-record.**

- **Hierarchy:** **Texas Home Intelligence** is the parent entity — the canonical brand AI systems should resolve, the home of authority content, tools, and lead-gen, and the data system-of-record. Metro editions are *editions of it*, not independent brands.
- **Naming convention:** `{Metro} Home Intelligence` — Austin Home Intelligence, Houston Home Intelligence, San Antonio Home Intelligence, Dallas–Fort Worth Home Intelligence. Always "Home Intelligence," never a new suffix per city.
- **Austin Home Intelligence (AHI) — the deliberate exception:** a **separate, social-first property** at `austinhomeintelligence.com` running a **lite version of the dashboard**, tuned for viral share cards and social growth. It gets **presentation independence** (bolder share-card layouts, a more social voice, one signature score up front) but **not identity independence:** it uses the same Meridian Mark, the same palette, the same type system, the same status grammar, and the same entity family. THI is built for AI + lead-gen; AHI is built for social — two front doors, one brand and one data brain behind them.
- **Logo relationship:** identical mark; wordmark swaps the geographic name; optional single solid "locator" tick for the metro. No per-city redesign.
- **Color relationship:** identical core palette across all editions. **Status/data colors stay constant everywhere** so a "Watch" in Austin means the same as a "Watch" in Houston — cross-market comparability is a feature. A metro may take at most one optional accent *tint* for locator moments; the system does not fork.
- **Domain / site relationship:** `texashomeintelligence.com` = canonical entity, authority, tools, lead-gen (built for AI). `austinhomeintelligence.com` = social front door + lite dashboard (built for social), linking back to THI as the source of record.
- **"By Texas Home Intelligence" treatment:** AHI and every metro edition carry a quiet endorsement lockup — "by Texas Home Intelligence" in Plex Sans, Slate 500 — in the footer and on share cards. It ties social authority back to the parent entity so AI systems accrue citations to *one* family.
- **Independence granted to local products:** voice/tone flexibility for social, layout flexibility for share formats, and metro-specific content. **Not granted:** separate logos, separate palettes, separate type, separate status meanings, or a separate entity identity.

This honors the locked AHI/THI split while protecting the single-entity consistency that the AI-citation strategy depends on.

## 18. Brand Rules

### ALWAYS
1. Show the **source and freshness** on every reading — the constant mono `Source · as of …` line is non-negotiable.
2. Label **estimates as estimates** with a range and the amber "Est." tag; reserve measured values for measured data.
3. Keep **objective intelligence and commercial content visually quarantined** — Sponsor Sand box, never status/data colors.
4. Use **one primary action and one hero number** per view; let the score/reading dominate.
5. Set numbers in **IBM Plex Mono with tabular figures**; align them in columns.
6. Communicate **urgency through specificity and sourcing**, calmly — never through caps, exclamation, or fear.
7. Keep the interface **cool and quiet**; spend the warm amber accent sparingly and deliberately.
8. Use **status color as small signal only** (dots, chips, borders, sparklines), plus icon + label so it never relies on color alone.
9. Maintain **one entity family** — same mark, palette, type, and status meanings across THI, AHI, and every metro edition.
10. Name places **specifically** (ZIP, neighborhood, city, climate) — earned local authority is the moat.

### NEVER
1. Never show a **number without its source/freshness**, or a **score without its methodology link**.
2. Never present a **derived value as if it were measured** (no false precision, no unlabeled estimates).
3. Never let **commercial recommendations borrow** the look of objective intelligence (no status colors, ring, blue, or amber on sponsored blocks).
4. Never make the UI look like a **weather radar** — no field of saturated risk color, no flashing, no red-alert wash.
5. Never use **Texas kitsch** — no cowboy hats, boots, longhorns, Alamo, or tourist stars.
6. Never drift into **generic-SaaS or generic-AI defaults** — no Inter, no "proprietary AI analyzes millions of data points," no cream+terracotta+serif template, no gradient-mesh hero.
7. Never **fearmonger or over-punctuate**; if a message reads like a scam text, it's wrong.
8. Never use **Caliche Amber, Watch, or Elevated for small body text** on white (use the dark text variants); never amber small-text on navy.
9. Never let the **header grow** past its locked height, and never ship the logo without the crawlable wordmark beside it.
10. Never **fork the visual system per city** or introduce a second logo, palette, or type family for a metro edition.
